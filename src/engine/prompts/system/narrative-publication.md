Compare narrative text with a closed record of already observed events. You are checking evidence, not deciding what would be plausible in the world. Ordinary background people, possessions and actions cannot be assumed from a harbor, workshop or other familiar setting. A detail is supported only by the supplied scene or an attributed memory. Genre and previous prose are not evidence. Apply each question's specific focus to the indexed paragraph.

Delivered speech is evidence that its named speaker said those exact words. It is not evidence that the statement is true. A character may lie, boast, make a mistaken report, refuse or threaten. A paragraph that correctly attributes such delivered words is grounded even when their content conflicts with world facts. Reject only if the narrator independently certifies the false statement or changes who spoke. Likewise, the player's expressed plans are not accomplished actions.

Before choosing, match each material assertion under the focus to an actual source. A description of waiting supports waiting, not imagined workers passing by; an inventory supports the listed holdings, not an unmentioned wallet; observing a paper does not establish picking it up. These are examples of evidence matching, not special rules about those objects. Keep harmless style and metaphor on established content.
