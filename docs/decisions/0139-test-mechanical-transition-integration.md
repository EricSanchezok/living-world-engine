# Test Mechanical Transition Integration

## Status

Accepted
Class: feature

## Context and Problem Statement

Independent transition probes can distinguish formal output failures from source-semantic failures, but they do not execute the engine's downstream causal verification, observation checks and repair. The gameplay objective concerns accepted full-world behavior and cost. An experimental interval-description interface also adds generated semantic evidence whose reliability is not established.

## Decision Drivers

Test the actual engine, retain final semantic vetoes, select demonstrated mechanical transformations, avoid stronger models and extra online critics, and keep unqualified research interfaces out of the gameplay candidate.

## Considered Options

- Require a raw transition probe to settle every semantic question before any integrated diagnostic.
- Integrate the full experimental interval-description interface.
- Integrate only mechanical representations and test the complete existing verification and repair path.

## Decision Outcome

The isolated gameplay candidate selects mechanical transition representations and retains the canonical summary/effect contract. The independent research probe explicitly selects the interval-description interface. Final source-bound state and behavior review remains the acceptance boundary after the existing engine checks. [Specification 0060](../specs/0060-integrated-transition-gameplay-diagnostic.md) owns rollout and verification. [Specification 0063](../specs/0063-canonical-transition-evidence.md) extends the same evidence view and explicit source cardinality to canonical single-request transitions without changing their output representation.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

Raw-probe qualification localizes generation errors but cannot establish how the existing verifier and repair recover them, or whether complete-world effects are credible. Integrating all research fields preserves more diagnostic prose but adds unqualified semantic generation and output burden. Mechanical integration tests the real pipeline with fewer generated fields and preserves exact inputs and canonical effects. It still depends on model behavior, so neither structural success nor the model verifier alone permits gameplay acceptance.
